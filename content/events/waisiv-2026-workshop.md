## Overview

The **I Workshop on Artificial Intelligence for Software Integration and Variability (WAISIV)** was held from **April 11 to 13, 2026**, at the **Bitingui Praia Hotel**, in **Japaratinga**, Alagoas, Brazil. The workshop gathered leading researchers from Brazilian and German universities working on applying Artificial Intelligence techniques to improve the productivity and resulting quality of Software Engineering tasks related to **Software Integration** (merge conflict detection and resolution tools, etc.) and **Software Variability** (in requirements, design, tests, code, etc.).

The workshop was sponsored and organized by the **National Institute of Science and Technology in Software Engineering (INES)** and the **Informatics Center (CIn)** of the **Federal University of Pernambuco (UFPE)**.

More details, including slides and photos, are available on the [workshop website](https://waisiv.cin.ufpe.br/jnamaral.github.io/WAISIV/index.html).

## Goals

- Discuss recent progress in research topics in AI applied to Software Integration and Variability.
- Foster research collaboration between Brazilian and German institutions.
- Promote interpersonal interactions among participants, stimulating lasting scientific cooperation networks.

  <h3 class="lecturer-name">Sandro Schulze</h3>
  <p class="lecturer-affil">Anhalt University of Applied Sciences, Germany</p>
  <p class="lecturer-talk">Better Software, Better World? Challenges and Promises of Sustainable Software Development</p>
  <details class="lecturer-abstract">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>Software has been a driving force behind technology and ubiquitous long before the widespread adoption of large language models. Compared to other technologies, however, very little is known about the sustainability of software. While aspects such as energy consumption and performance are widely discussed, the software development process itself remains almost unexplored in terms of sustainability. This talk introduces the concept of sustainability in the context of software, highlights the limitations of current research, addresses various aspects of sustainability in the context of software development, and presents recent work on software sustainability anti-patterns. The talk also aims to stimulate discussion on how the other topics of the workshop relate to and can contribute to sustainable software development.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="lecturer-abstract">
    <summary>Bio</summary>
    <p>Sandro Schulze is a Professor of Software Engineering at Anhalt University of Applied Sciences, Germany. His research focuses on the evolution and quality assurance of software systems, reverse engineering variability, applied machine learning for software analytics, and sustainability in software engineering. He was previously a postdoctoral researcher at TU Braunschweig and a lecturer at Otto-von-Guericke University Magdeburg, and held temporary professorships at TU Hamburg, the University of Potsdam, and TU Braunschweig. He holds a Ph.D. in Computer Science from Otto-von-Guericke University Magdeburg (2013). He has authored over seventy peer-reviewed publications, and his work has received three Most Influential Paper Awards (Programming'23, GPCE'23, VaMoS'25).</p>

  <h3 class="lecturer-name">Norbert Siegmund</h3>
  <p class="lecturer-affil">Leipzig University, Germany</p>
  <p class="lecturer-talk">The Promise of Tailor-Made Software: From Tale to Reality</p>
  <details class="lecturer-abstract">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>This talk critically reviews the promises research has made about end-user-driven configuration and optimization of configurable software systems, contrasting the optimizations achieved in lab experiments with what is actually observed in the wild. As a solution, it proposes using agents to bridge the gap from researchers and lab settings to local configuration at the user side, thereby exploiting further optimization potential. As this research is ongoing, the talk ends with open questions and challenges yet to be addressed.</p>

## Outcomes

The first edition of WAISIV established itself as a valuable space for scientific exchange between Brazilian and German researchers. As a concrete outcome of the planning discussions, participants agreed on organizing **research visits to Germany** and developing **joint scientific articles** on the themes discussed. The organizing committee encourages future editions of the workshop, possibly expanding its scope to additional partner institutions and introducing a peer-reviewed submission track.
